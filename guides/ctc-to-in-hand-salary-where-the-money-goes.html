CTC to in-hand salary: where the money goes

A ₹12 lakh CTC does not mean ₹1 lakh in your account every month. This guide follows one package from the offer letter to the bank account, shows why basic salary changes your take-home, and lists what to ask before you accept an offer. Updated 25 November 2026.

CTC is not your salary

CTC, cost to company, is everything your employer spends on you in a year. It is not the amount that lands in your bank account. Two offers with the same CTC can leave you with quite different monthly take-home, because the split inside the CTC is not the same. This guide follows a CTC of ₹12,00,000 from the offer letter to the bank account, using the same simple rules throughout.

How a CTC is split up

Assume basic salary is 40% of CTC, the employee lives in a metro city (so house rent allowance, HRA, is 50% of basic), provident fund is 12% of basic, and gratuity is set aside at about 4.81% of basic. Special allowance is simply whatever is left over:

Part of the CTCPer yearHow it is worked out
Basic salary₹4,80,00040% of CTC
HRA₹2,40,00050% of basic (metro)
Special allowance₹3,99,312the remainder
Employer’s PF₹57,60012% of basic
Gratuity₹23,0884.81% of basic
Total (the CTC)₹12,00,000

Two of those lines never reach your bank account in the year they are counted. The employer’s PF goes into your provident fund account. Gratuity is set aside for you and is usually paid only after five years of continuous service.

From CTC to the bank account

Take away the employer’s PF and the gratuity and you have the gross salary, the figure at the top of a payslip: 12,00,000 − 57,600 − 23,088 = ₹11,19,312 a year, or ₹93,276 a month. Then the deductions come off:

  • your own PF, another 12% of basic: ₹57,600 a year;
  • professional tax, a small state levy, often about ₹200 a month and different from state to state: ₹2,400 a year.

That leaves ₹10,59,312 a year, or about ₹88,276 a month before income tax. A CTC of ₹12 lakh divided by 12 is ₹1,00,000, so about ₹11,724 a month, or 11.7%, never arrives as spendable money, even before any income tax.

Why a higher basic lowers take-home

Basic salary drives the PF on both sides and the gratuity, so the share of CTC that is basic changes the take-home even when the CTC does not. For the same ₹12 lakh:

Basic as a share of CTCMonthly take-home before income tax
30%₹91,157
40%₹88,276
50%₹85,395

Each ten-point step in basic costs ₹2,881 a month, which is ₹34,572 a year. That money is not lost. It goes into your provident fund (both halves) and into gratuity. A higher basic is forced saving; a lower basic is more cash now. Which is better depends on you, but it is worth knowing which one the offer is giving you.

When PF is capped

Some employers work PF on a capped amount of ₹1,800 a month for each side, instead of 12% of the whole basic. On the same ₹12 lakh CTC with 40% basic, the take-home becomes about ₹94,276 a month, ₹6,000 more than at the full 12%. The offer letter or payslip says which way your PF is set up, and it is one of the first things to ask.

What this leaves out

  • Income tax. These figures are before income tax. How much tax you pay depends on your income and the regime you choose, and it is deducted by your employer as TDS.
  • Variable pay and bonuses. If part of the CTC is a bonus that is not guaranteed, your monthly figure will be lower than the fixed part suggests.
  • Other items. Insurance, NPS, food coupons and reimbursements can sit inside a CTC and change the picture. The payslip is the authority.

This guide is arithmetic with stated assumptions, not advice on any job offer. Check the figures with your employer’s HR team.

Questions to ask before you accept an offer

  1. Ask for the full breakup: basic, HRA, special allowance, PF, gratuity and any variable pay.
  2. Ask whether PF is on the full basic or capped.
  3. Ask whether gratuity and variable pay are inside the CTC or on top of it.
  4. Work out the monthly take-home before tax, and compare offers on that, not on CTC.
