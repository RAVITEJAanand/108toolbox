What the number means

        
Flesch Reading Ease runs from about 0 to 100, and higher is easier.
           It is built from two things only: how long your sentences are, and
           how many syllables your words have.

        
    
          
  • 90–100 very easy — a young child
              could read it
    • 
          
  • 60–70 plain English — what most
              newspapers aim at, and a good target for anything public
    • 
          
  • 30–50 difficult — academic or
              official writing
    • 
          
  • under 30 very difficult — a degree helps
    • 
        

        
The grade beside it is the American school year needed to read the
           text comfortably. Grade 8 is roughly a thirteen-year-old, and is the
           usual advice for writing meant for everybody.

        

        
        
What the number cannot see

        
It counts syllables and full stops. It does not read. So it does not
           know whether your text is clear, only whether it is
           short.

        
You can get a perfect score out of nonsense: chop every sentence in
           two, replace long words with short ones, and the number climbs while
           the meaning falls apart. Treat it as a smoke alarm, not a
           judge — a low score is a reason to reread, never a reason to
           start hacking sentences in half.

        
It also has nothing to say about technical writing. A page about
           haemoglobin has to say haemoglobin, and no rewording makes that word
           shorter. A specification full of necessary long words will score
           badly and be exactly as clear as it should be.

        

        
        
Why the sentence list matters more

        
The single number tells you there is a problem somewhere. The list
           underneath tells you where. Almost always a handful of very long
           sentences are dragging the whole text down, and splitting those
           three sentences does more than rewriting every other word on the
           page.

        

        
        
Syllables are counted by a rule of thumb

        
English spelling has no reliable relationship to its sounds, so
           there is no way to count syllables exactly without a dictionary of
           every word. This page uses the usual set of rules — count the
           vowel groups, drop a silent e, and so on — and
           gets most words right and some words wrong. Every readability tool
           does the same thing, which is why two of them rarely agree
           exactly.

        

        
        
Frequently asked questions

        

          
What score should I aim for?

          
Around 60 to 70 for anything a general audience reads, which is roughly grade 8. Official and academic writing sits far lower and is not necessarily wrong for its readers.

        

        

          
Why does another site give me a different score?

          
Because syllable counting is a guess. English spelling does not reliably tell you how a word sounds, so every tool uses slightly different rules and lands on slightly different numbers. Treat the score as a band, not a measurement.

        

        

          
Is a low score always bad?

          
No. Technical writing often has to use long words, and shortening them would lose the meaning. A low score means 'this will be hard work to read', which is sometimes a fair price and sometimes a warning.

        

        

          
Can I cheat it?

          
Easily, and that is worth knowing. Splitting every sentence and swapping in short words raises the score whether or not the writing improves. The number is a prompt to look, not a target to hit.

        

        

          
What counts as a sentence?

          
Text ending in a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark. Abbreviations with full stops in them will be miscounted as sentence endings, which nudges the score - another reason to read the number loosely.

        

        

          
Is my text sent anywhere?

          
No. Everything happens in JavaScript inside your own browser. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is saved - close the tab and it is gone.
