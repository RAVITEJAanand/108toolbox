Mean, median and mode are three different questions

        
People say “average” and almost always mean the
          mean: add everything up, divide by how many there are.
          It is the right tool most of the time, and it has one weakness that
          matters.


        
The mean is dragged around by extreme values. Nine people earning
          30,000 and one earning 5,00,000 have a mean salary of 77,000 — a
          number that describes nobody in the room. The median,
          the middle value once the list is sorted, stays at 30,000 and tells the
          honest story.


        
The mode is the value that appears most often. It is
          the only one of the three that works on things you cannot add up, like
          shirt sizes or survey answers, and it is the right question when you want
          to know what is most common rather than what is typical.


        
A quick rule: if the mean and the median on this page are far
          apart, your list has outliers, and the median is probably the
          number you should quote.

        

        
        
Range and standard deviation

        
Range is simply the largest number minus the smallest.
          It tells you how spread out the list is, using only two values, which
          makes it quick but fragile — one freak number changes it entirely.


        
Standard deviation answers the same question more
          carefully: on average, how far is each number from the mean? A small
          standard deviation means the values huddle close together; a large one
          means they are scattered.


        
There are two of them, and this page shows both. The
          population standard deviation (σ) divides by how
          many numbers there are, and treats your list as the whole group
          — every student in the class. The sample
          standard deviation (s) divides by one fewer, and is the one to use
          when your list is a sample from a bigger group, as in most
          statistics coursework. The sample figure is never the smaller of the
          two, and with a single number it is left blank: one value cannot
          show how spread out a group is.

        

        
        
Frequently asked questions

        

          
What is the difference between mean, median and mode?

          
The mean is the total divided by how many numbers there are. The median is the middle number once they are sorted. The mode is the number that appears most often. They answer different questions, and when a list has a few very large or very small values they can be far apart.

        

        

          
When should I use the median instead of the mean?

          
Whenever a few extreme values would drag the mean away from what is typical. Salaries are the classic case: one very large salary lifts the mean above what almost everyone earns, while the median stays with the middle person. If the mean and median on this page are far apart, that is your signal.

        

        

          
How should I separate my numbers?

          
However you like. Commas, spaces, new lines, tabs or semicolons all work, and you can mix them. Negative numbers and decimals are fine. A symbol in front or a unit behind is fine: ₹500, 50% and 3.5kg all count. Anything else that is not a number is skipped, and the page names it so you can see what was left out.

        

        

          
Is my data sent anywhere?

          
No. The arithmetic happens in JavaScript inside your own browser. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is saved — close the tab and it is gone.
