Cron Expression Parser

Paste a cron line and find out what it really means — including the one rule about days that surprises almost everybody the first time it costs them a night's sleep.

In plain words

—Fields
—Runs per day
—Shown in
Next five runs

The five fields

        
In order: minute, hour, day of the month, month, day of the
           week. So 30 5 * * 1-5 is half past five in the
           morning, Monday to Friday.

        
Each field takes a number, a list (1,15), a range
           (9-17), a step (*/15, meaning every
           fifteenth), or * for every value. Months and weekdays
           also take names: JAN, MON. Sunday is
           0, and also 7, which exists because
           different systems disagreed and both had to keep working.


        
The rule that catches everyone

        
If you restrict both the day of the month and the
           day of the week, cron runs when either one matches
           — not both.

        
0 0 13 * 5 does not mean “Friday the
           13th”. It means every 13th of the month, and also every
           Friday — which is roughly sixty-five runs a year instead
           of the one or two you had in mind. Press the trap above and
           read the next five dates; they will not all be Fridays.

        
It is not a bug, it is written down in the manual, and it still
           catches people with twenty years of experience. If you genuinely
           need Friday the 13th, you schedule every 13th and check the weekday
           inside your own script.


        
Which clock does it use?

        
The machine's, not yours. A cron line on a server set to UTC runs at
           those times in UTC, and the times below are shown in
           your zone only so you have something to compare against.
           This is worth checking before you decide a job did not run.

        
Daylight saving makes it stranger still. On the morning the clocks
           go forward, a job scheduled inside the hour that never happens may
           not run at all; when they go back, an hour repeats and a job can run
           twice. Anything that must happen exactly once is safer scheduled
           outside that window — or written so that running twice does no
           harm, which is the better habit anyway.


        
The shortcuts

        
@yearly, @monthly, @weekly,
           @daily and @hourly all work and are
           expanded here so you can see what they really are. They are easier
           to read than the equivalent five fields and mean exactly the same
           thing.


        
Frequently asked questions

        

          
Why does my job run on days I did not ask for?

          
Almost certainly because you restricted both the day of the month and the day of the week. When both are set, cron runs if either matches, not if both do. Set one of them back to * and the surprise stops.

        

        

          
What time zone are the next runs in?

          
Yours, as your browser reports it, purely so the dates mean something to you. The actual job runs in whatever zone the server is set to, which is very often UTC and is the first thing to check when a job seems to run at the wrong time.

        

        

          
Is Sunday 0 or 7?

          
Both. Different implementations disagreed long ago and the answer was to accept either, so 0 and 7 both mean Sunday. Saturday is always 6.

        

        

          
Does it support seconds?

          
No, and standard cron does not either. Six-field expressions with seconds at the front belong to particular schedulers - Quartz, some libraries - and are not what a Unix crontab reads.

        

        

          
Can I check whether my expression is valid without running it?

          
That is what this page is for. If it cannot be parsed you get a message saying which field is wrong, and if it can, the next five dates tell you far more than the expression does.

        

        

          
Is my text sent anywhere?

          
No. Everything happens in JavaScript inside your own browser. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is saved - close the tab and it is gone.
