What this page cannot do

        
It cannot tell you whether a name is free. Checking would mean asking
           every site you might join, one at a time, from your browser —
           which they block, and which would tell each of them what you are
           about to sign up for. So the honest answer is to generate a handful
           of names you like and try them where you actually want the
           account.

        
The possible names figure is the size of the pool
           these settings can draw from, not a promise of availability. A big
           pool is still the right thing to want: a two-word name out of
           thousands of combinations is far likelier to be free than
           anil123.

        

        
        
Which digits not to use

        
Your birth year is the worst choice, and it is the most common one.
           It hands your age to everyone who reads your name, and it is among
           the first things guessed when somebody tries to answer a security
           question or reset a password. The same goes for a birthday written
           as four digits.

        
If you want digits, let them be meaningless. Two random ones are
           enough to get past “already taken” without saying
           anything about you.

        

        
        
A username is not a password

        
It is public, permanent and hard to change. Most sites let you
           change a password in seconds and a username never, or once.

        
Which leads to the thing worth thinking about for a minute: using the
           same distinctive username everywhere makes every one of your accounts
           findable from any one of them. There are sites that do nothing but
           check a name against hundreds of services at once. Sometimes that is
           exactly what you want — one recognisable handle. Sometimes it
           is the last thing you want, and then the answer is a different name
           per account, which is a decision to make on purpose rather than by
           habit.

        

        
        
Why words rather than random letters

        
Because you will have to read it out over a phone, type it on a
           television remote, and recognise it in a list a year from now.
           swiftotter survives all three;
           xk7f2qd survives none of them. Random letters are the
           right answer for a password, where nobody should ever have to say it
           aloud.

        

        
        
Frequently asked questions

        

          
Can you check whether the username is available?

          
No, and no page that runs entirely in your browser can. Sites block that kind of automated checking, and doing it would mean telling every one of them what you are about to sign up for. Generate a few you like and try them where you want the account.

        

        

          
Should I put my birth year in it?

          
No. It tells everyone your age and it is one of the first guesses anyone makes when trying to reset your password or answer a security question. If you want digits, use meaningless ones.

        

        

          
Is it safe to use the same username on every site?

          
It is safe in the sense that a username is not a secret, but it links all those accounts together - anyone who finds one can look for the rest, and there are tools built to do exactly that. If you want accounts kept separate, use a different name on each.

        

        

          
Why are the names made of real words?

          
Because you have to say it aloud, type it on awkward keyboards, and recognise it later. Words survive that; random letters do not. Random letters are the right answer for a password, which nobody should ever have to read out.

        

        

          
What does 'possible names' actually count?

          
How many different names your current settings could produce - the word lists multiplied together, times the range of the digits. It is the size of the pool, not a count of free names. Adding a second word or one more digit multiplies it.

        

        

          
Is my text sent anywhere?

          
No. Everything happens in JavaScript inside your own browser. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is saved - close the tab and it is gone.
