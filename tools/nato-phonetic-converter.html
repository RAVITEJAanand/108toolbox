Why the spellings look wrong

        
It is Alfa, not Alpha, and Juliett
          with two t's. Neither is a typo. The alphabet was agreed
          internationally, and it had to work for speakers of many languages over
          a poor radio link. Ph is not read as an f sound
          everywhere, so Alpha became Alfa. A French speaker would treat a single
          final t as silent, so Juliet gained a second one.


        
The numbers have the same history. Tick Aviation
          numbers and nine becomes niner, so it cannot
          be confused with the German nein; three becomes
          tree, because the th sound does not exist in
          many languages; four becomes fower and five becomes
          fife, both gaining clarity over a hiss of static.
          Outside aviation, ordinary number words are fine and are the default
          here.


        
The everyday use is simpler than radio work: reading a booking
          reference to an airline, a policy number to an insurer, an email
          address to someone typing it in. The pairs that go wrong most are
          M and N, B and D, S and
          F, and P and T — one word each and the
          ambiguity disappears.


        
Frequently asked questions

        

          
Why is it spelled Alfa and Juliett rather than Alpha and Juliet?

          
Both spellings are deliberate. Alfa avoids the ph, which is not read as an f sound in every language. Juliett carries a second t so that French speakers do not treat the final t as silent. The alphabet was designed to survive a bad radio link in any accent, and the odd spellings are part of that.

        

        

          
What are Tree, Fower and Niner?

          
They are the aviation pronunciations of three, four and nine. Nine becomes niner so it cannot be heard as the German nein, three loses the th sound that many speakers do not have, and four gains a syllable so it is not confused with for. Tick the aviation option to use them.

        

        

          
When is this actually useful?

          
Any time a letter matters and the line is bad: reading a booking reference or PNR to an airline, giving a policy or case number over the phone, dictating an email address, or confirming a vehicle registration. M and N, B and D, and S and F are the pairs that get misheard most.

        

        

          
Is my text sent anywhere?

          
No. The conversion happens in JavaScript inside your own browser. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is saved — close the tab and the text is gone.
